# US Superstore Sales Analysis — Power BI



## 1. Project Overview

Superstore is a fictional retail business operating across the United States, selling a wide variety of products. This project uses Power BI to examine its sales data, understand profitability, and identify areas where business performance could be improved.

The analysis focuses on regional sales, product performance, customer segments, seasonal trends, and shipping preferences to derive actionable business insights.

### Questions Explored

- Which regions, states, and cities generate the highest sales?
- Which products perform well, and which ones have profitability issues?
- How do sales and profit vary over time?
- Which customer segments contribute the most to sales activity?
- Which shipping methods are used most frequently?
- Where can inventory management and operational performance improve?
- What do the business KPIs reveal about overall performance?

## 2. Dataset Overview

The dashboards cover sales performance across the displayed years, from 2019 to 2022. The dataset includes sales, profit, discounts, product quantities, customer segments, geographical information, and shipping methods.

Dataset cleaning details and the exact number of records should be added only after verification against the original data.

**Overall performance**

- Superstore recorded **2.30M in sales** and **286.40K in profit**, with an average discount of **15.62%** across the period shown.
- Although the sales figure is substantial, revenue alone does not explain business profitability. Product margins and the impact of discounts also need to be considered.

**Regional sales distribution**

- The **West region leads with approximately 725.5K in sales**, followed by the **East at around 678.8K**.
- Together, these two regions contribute approximately **61% of total sales**, making them important markets to maintain and strengthen.
- The **South records approximately 391.7K**, the lowest sales figure among the four regions shown. Lower demand, product availability, order volume, and average order value are possible areas to investigate before increasing marketing expenditure.

**Product-level performance**

- **Phones lead sales in the sub-category chart**, while Tables generate sales but show negative profit.
- Tables should be examined more closely by reviewing product costs, discounts, shipping expenses, and selling prices.
- Since product sales vary considerably, promotional and inventory decisions should consider profit contribution rather than revenue alone.

**Monthly trends**

- **November records the highest monthly sales at approximately 356.5K**, with September also performing strongly.
- February shows considerably lower sales activity.
- These patterns can help with inventory and delivery preparation ahead of peak periods. The February decline should be checked across individual years before deciding whether promotions are necessary.

**Customer and shipping patterns**

- The **Consumer segment accounts for approximately 19.5K items sold**, ahead of Corporate and Home Office.
- The company should maintain demand in its largest segment while testing targeted offers for smaller segments and evaluating their impact on profit and repeat orders.
- **Standard Class accounts for approximately 23K items** in the shipping-mode view, making it the most-used shipping method.
- Its delivery times, costs, and late-order rates should be monitored to ensure that high usage is supported by reliable and cost-effective service.

**Sales and profit over time**

- The yearly charts show sales and profit increasing throughout the displayed period.
- However, rising sales do not necessarily indicate that profitability is improving at the same rate. Order count, average order value, and profit margin should be evaluated alongside revenue.
- The Corporate segment's profit does not appear to increase as strongly as its sales in the final year shown.
- Reviewing its product mix, discounting practices, and associated costs could help explain this difference before further investment in the segment.

**Shipping performance**

- Standard Class contributes the largest share of sales and profit in the shipping charts, highlighting its importance to the existing delivery model.
- Shipping options should be compared based on cost, delivery speed, and reliability to determine whether the most-used method also provides an effective customer experience.
- Monitoring shipping expenses and late deliveries over time can reveal whether selected orders would benefit from alternative shipping methods.

**Order concentration by city**

- **New York City has the highest order count in the displayed table**, followed by Los Angeles.
- Both cities should be included in regular reviews of product availability, fulfilment, and delivery performance.
- Comparing city-level sales, profit, and order trends can help identify where demand is increasing and where additional inventory or marketing investment may be justified.

## 4. Key Recommendations

- **Improve product profitability:** Investigate Tables and other weak-profit products by reviewing costs, discounts, returns, and selling prices.
- **Evaluate discount effectiveness:** Determine whether discounts generate additional profit or simply reduce earnings per order.
- **Optimise inventory allocation:** Prepare for peak months and align stock with product demand across different locations.
- **Strengthen regional performance:** Maintain availability in the West and East while investigating the factors contributing to lower sales in the South.
- **Monitor shipping efficiency:** Track Standard Class delivery times, costs, and late orders, and compare its performance with alternative shipping methods.
- **Measure sustainable growth:** Review sales alongside profit margin, order count, average order value, and repeat orders.

## 5. Conclusion

The dashboards highlight Superstore's strong sales performance while revealing opportunities to improve profitability and operational efficiency. Product-level profit analysis, more effective discounting, demand-based inventory planning, and shipping performance monitoring can support better business decisions.
